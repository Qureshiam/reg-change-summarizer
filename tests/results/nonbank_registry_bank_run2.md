## Bottom line
This final rule rescinds the CFPB's nonbank registry rule. It does not apply directly to a bank or credit union, because the registry covered nonbanks and excluded insured depository institutions and insured credit unions. It has been in effect since October 29, 2025, so the only action is a short confirmation that no nonbank affiliate or subsidiary of yours still treats the registry as an open obligation.

## What is changing
- **Old requirement (the "NBR Rule", 89 FR 56028, published July 8, 2024, effective September 16, 2024):**
  - Covered nonbanks subject to certain public agency or court orders had to register those orders with the Bureau.
  - Bureau-supervised registered entities had to file an annual written statement signed by a designated "attesting executive."
  - The Bureau was authorized to publish the registry information.
- **New requirement:** All of this is rescinded in full. The Bureau also rescinded the related subpart A, §§ 1092.200-201 and 206, and appendix A, because they are no longer applicable.
- **Why the Bureau acted:** It concluded the costs were not justified by speculative and unquantified benefits, and that the registry was not a necessary monitoring tool.
- **Related earlier statement:** The document notes an April 11, 2025 Bureau announcement that it would not prioritize enforcement or supervision for entities missing future NBR Rule deadlines.

## Does it apply to this servicer?
**Out of scope directly.**
- The document says the rule "will only apply to nonbanks" and "will have no direct impacts on any insured depository institution or insured credit union."
- The summary of the NBR Rule's definition of "covered nonbank" lists exceptions for insured depository institutions, insured credit unions, and persons covered solely as related persons.
- Your profile is a bank or credit union with more than 5,000 loans. The document sets no size-based distinction relevant to servicing portfolio size. The only size threshold is the $5 million annual receipts line for supervised nonbanks.

**Possible indirect relevance.**
- The document records commenter objections that the NBR Rule reached nonbank affiliates of insured depository institutions and credit union service organizations (CUSOs).
- The text does not say whether such affiliates were covered. Whether any affiliate of yours was a covered nonbank is Not stated in the document.
- The document says affiliated institutions "might experience indirect benefits" from relief to their nonbank affiliates.

## What is not changing
- The underlying public agency and court orders remain in force, along with every obligation they impose. Only the registration, attestation, and publication layers are removed.
- The Bureau says it can still conduct risk-based follow-up examinations to assess compliance with orders, and investigate credible allegations of consumer harm.
- The Bureau and other agencies keep their existing supervision and enforcement roles under Federal consumer financial law.
- NMLS and other existing public sources of order information are unaffected. The Bureau relies on them as existing mechanisms. The document does not state any change to state licensing or NMLS disclosure requirements.
- The document is silent on any remaining Bureau handling of registrations already submitted. This is Not stated in the document.

## Recommended next steps
1. Confirm with Legal whether any nonbank affiliate, subsidiary, or CUSO was or is a "covered nonbank" under the former rule, and whether any registrations were filed.
2. If so, stop related reporting workstreams and update compliance calendars, attesting-executive designations, and policy references.
3. Confirm that no other order-related obligations were commingled with NBR tracking. The orders themselves remain enforceable.
4. Document the determination that the rule is out of scope for the bank, for the regulatory change log.
5. Check state-level order-reporting or licensing requirements that may continue independently (see Items to verify).
