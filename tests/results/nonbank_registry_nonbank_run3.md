## Bottom line
This final rule rescinds the CFPB's Nonbank Registry rule in full, so the registration, annual attestation, and publication requirements no longer apply. As a nonbank servicer, you are within the class of entities the rescission relieves. The rule has been in effect since October 29, 2025, so the work is housekeeping rather than new compliance build. The key action is to confirm whether you registered or designated an attesting executive, then retire those obligations from your compliance inventory and calendars. Obligations under any underlying agency or court orders are not affected.

## What is changing
**Old requirement (the "NBR Rule," 89 FR 56028, published July 8, 2024, effective September 16, 2024).** The rule had three parts:
- **Registration:** Covered nonbanks subject to certain public agency or court orders had to register with the CFPB and submit the orders and related information. The document describes a lighter one-time option for orders published on NMLS Consumer Access. The document recites registration deadlines of January 14, 2025; April 14, 2025; and July 14, 2025, depending on entity category.
- **Annual written statements:** Bureau-supervised registered entities had to designate an "attesting executive" and submit an annual signed statement on compliance with covered orders. The document describes this as applying to entities at or above $5 million in annual receipts, and says it runs for at least 10 years.
- **Publication:** The Bureau was authorized, but not required, to publish registry information.

**New requirement.** All three parts are rescinded and part 1092 is removed and reserved. The Bureau's stated reasons are as follows:
- Costs outweigh speculative, unquantified benefits.
- The registry is largely duplicative of NMLS and other public sources.
- The registry is not a necessary tool for monitoring risk.
- The Bureau bears its own cost of maintaining the system.

The document says the Bureau announced on April 11, 2025 that it would not prioritize enforcement or supervision for entities missing future NBR deadlines. That was an announcement, not a rule change. The rescission is what removes the legal requirement.

## Does it apply to this servicer?
**Partly in scope, and in a favorable way.** The rule relieves nonbank covered persons. The document lists "extending credit and servicing loans" among the covered consumer financial products and services.
- **Institution type:** You are a nonbank, which is the class the rescission relieves. The document says it has no direct impact on insured depository institutions or credit unions.
- **Portfolio size:** The document draws no distinction by portfolio size. The only size threshold it mentions is the NBR Rule's $5 million annual receipts threshold, which applied to the annual-statement requirement only. That threshold is now moot.
- **Not stated in the document or your profile:** Whether you have any covered orders, were Bureau-supervised, or ever registered or submitted a statement. These facts determine whether you have anything to wind down. Because the rule is rescinded, any such obligations no longer apply.

## What is not changing
- The underlying agency and court orders, and any obligations they impose on you, remain in force. The document states the rescission removes only the Bureau's registration, attestation, and publication layers.
- The Bureau's supervision, examination, and enforcement authority remains, including risk-based follow-up examinations on compliance with orders and investigations. The document cites these as the tools it will rely on instead.
- NMLS disclosures are not changed by this document. Commenters describe existing MU1 disclosure of orders for state licensing, and the document makes no change to that process.
- Federal consumer financial law and state law obligations are unaffected. The document does not address specific servicing rules.

## Recommended next steps
1. Establish facts. Determine whether you held any covered orders, created a Bureau registry account, registered an order, or designated an attesting executive. Document the answers.
2. Have Legal confirm that no NBR obligations remain, then remove them from policies, calendars, and obligation inventories.
3. Decide retention treatment for NBR-related records and filings, with Legal.
4. Confirm that tracking of obligations under your underlying orders, and NMLS and state reporting, is unchanged.
5. Notify any former attesting executive, vendors, and outside counsel, and close out related work.
