## Bottom line
This final rule is already in effect (since October 29, 2025) and fully rescinds the CFPB's nonbank registry rule. It is deregulatory and creates no new obligation. If the servicer registered or filed under that rule, or has a covered order, the main action is to confirm that registry processes are formally retired and that no other order obligations were dropped by mistake. The date-based priority is High, but the workload should be light.

## What is changing
**Old requirement (the "NBR Rule," 89 FR 56028, published July 8, 2024):**
- Nonbank covered persons subject to certain public agency and court orders had to register with the Bureau and submit the orders and related information.
- Bureau-supervised registered entities had to designate an "attesting executive." That executive signed an annual written statement describing oversight steps and attesting whether noncompliance with the order was identified. Records had to be kept for five years.
- The Bureau could publish registration information.
- Registrants had to file updates and corrections, such as within 90 days of an order's termination or modification and within 30 days of discovering an inaccuracy.

**New requirement:** The Bureau removes and reserves all of 12 CFR part 1092. That eliminates:
- the registration requirements, including the one-time NMLS-published-order option;
- the written-statement and attesting-executive requirements;
- the publication provisions;
- the general provisions, definitions, phased implementation dates, and appendix A (the list of state laws).

The Bureau gives these reasons: costs were not justified by speculative and unquantified benefits, the registry duplicated existing sources such as NMLS, and the Bureau can monitor orders without it.

## Does it apply to this servicer?
**Partly in scope, depending on facts the document does not give.**
- **Institution type:** The NBR Rule applied to nonbank covered persons, and the document lists "extending credit and servicing loans" among the covered activities. A nonbank servicer therefore falls on the affected side of the line. Insured depository institutions, insured credit unions, and related persons were excluded, and that does not apply here.
- **Portfolio size:** The document sets no tiers by portfolio size, so the over-5,000-loan profile does not change the analysis. The only size-related threshold it mentions is the $5 million annual receipts threshold in the NBR Rule's definition of supervised registered entities.
- **Practical effect:** The rescission matters only if the servicer was subject to a covered order, or if it started or completed an NBR registration. The document does not say whether this servicer has covered orders, whether it was a Bureau-supervised registered entity, or whether it registered. Those facts are internal and need to be confirmed.
- **Status of the old deadlines:** The prior registration deadlines (January 14, April 14, and July 14, 2025) are described in the document as having passed. The rescission removes the underlying requirements.

## What is not changing
- The document rescinds only part 1092. It does not purport to modify any underlying agency or court order, and the covered orders are described as already public. Any obligations in an order the servicer is subject to are not addressed by this rule.
- The Bureau states that it keeps its tools for risk-based follow-up examinations on compliance with orders and for investigations of credible allegations of tangible consumer harm. It also states that other federal and state agencies continue to monitor compliance with their own orders.
- The document describes NMLS reporting as an existing, separate mechanism. For example, a commenter described the MU1 disclosure of regulatory orders. This rule does not purport to change NMLS requirements, but the text does not confirm that either way.
- State law and state licensing: Not stated in the document.

## Recommended next steps
1. Confirm whether the servicer is subject to any covered orders or ever created an NBR account, registered, or submitted a filing or attestation.
2. Retire NBR-specific procedures, calendars, attesting-executive designations, and vendor or system support, with Legal sign-off.
3. Confirm that order-specific obligations and NMLS and state disclosure processes continue unchanged, so the rescission is not read as relief from them.
4. Decide on retention or disposition of NBR-related records, checking first for legal holds or examination requests.
5. Brief the senior leadership team and any former attesting executive, and note the change in the regulatory change log.
