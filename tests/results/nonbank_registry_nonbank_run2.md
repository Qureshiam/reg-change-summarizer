## Bottom line
The rule is final and has been in effect since October 29, 2025. It relieves nonbank servicers of the CFPB nonbank registry obligations (order registration, annual attesting-executive written statements, and registry publication) and creates no new obligation. It likely applies to this servicer as a nonbank, and the main action is to confirm that registry-related calendars, designations, and procedures are retired and that any past registry filings or gaps are accounted for.

## What is changing
**Old requirement (the "NBR Rule," 89 FR 56028, published July 8, 2024, effective September 16, 2024):**
- **Registration.** Covered nonbanks subject to covered orders had to register with the Bureau and submit the order and identifying information. A limited one-time option existed for NMLS-published covered orders.
- **Ongoing updates.** Registrants had to file updates and corrections.
- **Annual written statements.** Bureau-supervised registered entities had to designate an attesting executive and submit an annual signed statement on order compliance.
- **Publication.** The Bureau was authorized to publish registry information.
- **Registration deadlines.** The document lists deadlines of January 14, 2025, April 14, 2025, and July 14, 2025, depending on entity category.

**New requirement:** The Bureau is removing and reserving all of 12 CFR part 1092. This eliminates the registration, written-statement, and publication provisions, along with the general provisions, definitions, implementation dates, and the state-law appendix that supported them. No replacement requirement is created. The Bureau declined partial rescission and declined a general nonbank registration requirement.

## Does it apply to this servicer?
**Partly in scope, as a relief rule.**
- **Institution type.** The NBR Rule applied to nonbank covered persons, and "extending credit and servicing loans" is among the listed covered products and services. It excluded insured depository institutions, insured credit unions, and related persons. As a nonbank servicer, this servicer falls on the nonbank side of the line. Under the old rule it was potentially subject to registration if it had a covered order.
- **Portfolio size.** The document sets no loan-count threshold, so there is no small-servicer exemption tied to portfolio size. The more-than-5,000-loan profile does not change applicability.
- **Annual written statements.** These applied only to Bureau-supervised registered entities with $5 million or more in annual receipts from consumer financial products or services. Not stated in the document: whether this servicer was a supervised entity, had covered orders, or had registered.
- **Practical effect.** The old requirements are gone whether or not the servicer registered.

## What is not changing
- **Underlying orders.** The agency and court orders themselves, and any obligations they impose, are not affected. The document describes the registry as a reporting layer over public orders.
- **Bureau oversight.** The Bureau says it can still conduct risk-based follow-up examinations on compliance with orders and investigate credible allegations of consumer harm.
- **Other regulators.** Other Federal and State agencies continue to monitor compliance with their own orders.
- **NMLS and state licensing.** The document describes NMLS MU1 disclosures of orders as an existing separate mechanism. This rule does not rescind them.
- **Past conduct.** The rule does not say whether any legal consequence attaches to past noncompliance with the NBR Rule before rescission. See Items to verify.
