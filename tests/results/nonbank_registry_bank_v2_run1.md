## What is changing
- **Old requirement:** Covered nonbanks subject to certain public agency or court orders had to do three things:
  - register those orders with the Bureau;
  - for Bureau-supervised registered entities, submit an annual signed written statement from a designated "attesting executive";
  - accept that the Bureau could publish the registration information.
- **New requirement:** None. The Bureau removes and reserves all of 12 CFR part 1092. This eliminates the registration, written-statement and publication provisions, plus the general provisions, the phased implementation dates and the appendix of covered State laws.
- **Stated reasons:** The Bureau found the costs were not justified by speculative and unquantified benefits, that the registry duplicated existing sources such as NMLS, and that it was not necessary for monitoring risk.

## Does it apply to this servicer?
**Out of scope.** The document says the rule applies to nonbanks and defines "covered nonbank" to exclude insured depository institutions and insured credit unions. The Bureau's own analysis says the rule "will have no direct impacts on any insured depository institution or insured credit union."

Two points to note:
- The document says some institutions may see indirect effects. Examples are institutions with affiliated nonbanks that benefit from the relief, and institutions that compete with those nonbanks.
- The text also mentions commenter concerns about the old rule reaching nonbank affiliates of depository institutions and credit union service organizations (CUSOs). It excludes "related persons," and it does not say how a given affiliate would have been treated. Treat affiliate coverage as an item to verify.

This servicer's portfolio size (more than 5,000 loans) does not change the analysis. The rule has no size-based servicing thresholds.

## What is not changing
- The document does not change any servicing, loss mitigation, escrow, early intervention or foreclosure requirement. It does not discuss them.
- The document does not rescind any underlying public order. It removes only the duty to report orders to the Bureau's registry.
- The document describes NMLS as a separate, existing system. Nothing in the text changes NMLS reporting or State licensing obligations.
- The Bureau's supervision, enforcement and order-monitoring authority is described as continuing without the registry.
