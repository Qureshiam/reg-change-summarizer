## Bottom line
This final rule rescinds the CFPB's nonbank registry rule. That rule applied to nonbanks, and the document says it excludes insured depository institutions and insured credit unions, so it does not appear to impose or remove any obligation for this bank or credit union servicer. It has been in effect since October 29, 2025. The one action is to confirm that no nonbank affiliate or privately insured credit union entity in your corporate family had registry obligations, and to retire any registry tracking you set up.

## What is changing
**Old requirement (the 2024 NBR Rule, 89 FR 56028).** The rule contained three parts:
- **Registration.** Covered nonbanks subject to certain public agency and court orders had to register with the Bureau and submit the orders and related information. An NMLS-published order had a limited one-time registration option.
- **Annual written statement.** Bureau-supervised registered entities had to designate an attesting executive and submit an annual signed statement on compliance with covered orders.
- **Publication.** The Bureau was authorized to publish registry information.

**New requirement.** All of it is rescinded, and the document says the registry is no longer required. The rule removes and reserves all of part 1092, including subpart A, §§ 1092.200-201 and 206, and appendix A. The Bureau gives these reasons:
- The costs are not justified by speculative and unquantified benefits.
- The orders are already public.
- The registry duplicates NMLS and other sources.
- The Bureau can monitor orders without a registry.

## Does it apply to this servicer?
**Out of scope on the document's text, with two points to confirm.**
- The document says the rule "will only apply to nonbanks" and "will have no direct impacts on any insured depository institution or insured credit union." The NBR Rule's definition of "covered nonbank" excluded insured depository institutions and insured credit unions (fn. 7 and § XI.E). A bank or credit union servicer was not subject to the registry, and the rescission changes nothing for it directly.
- **Affiliates.** The document says the rescission may indirectly benefit nonbank affiliates of insured institutions. It also reports that bank trade groups objected to the NBR Rule's inclusion of nonbank affiliates. The text does not say precisely how affiliates were treated. It excludes a person who is a covered person "solely due to being a related person" (fn. 7), but it does not resolve how this applied to affiliates generally. If you have nonbank affiliates or subsidiaries that service or originate loans, ask whether they were treated as registrants.
- **Privately insured credit unions and CUSOs.** One commenter objected to the NBR Rule's coverage of CUSOs and privately insured credit unions. The document does not state whether they were covered. Your profile says "bank or credit union" and does not say whether the institution is federally insured.
- **Portfolio size.** The document sets no servicer-size distinction. The only size-related line is the $5 million annual receipts threshold for the attestation requirement, which applied only to Bureau-supervised nonbanks. Your more-than-5,000-loan portfolio is not relevant to this rule.

## What is not changing
- The orders themselves. The rule does not alter, vacate, or end any agency or court order, or any obligations under one. An order's own compliance and reporting terms remain in force.
- Existing laws. The Bureau and other agencies still enforce Federal and State consumer financial law, including UDAAP.
- Supervision and enforcement. The Bureau says it can still conduct risk-based follow-up examinations on order compliance and investigate credible allegations.
- NMLS. The document describes NMLS Consumer Access, with its MU1 disclosure of orders, as an existing mechanism. It was not changed by this rule.
- Other Bureau programs. The document does not address any servicing rule or any other Bureau reporting program.
