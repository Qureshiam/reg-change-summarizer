## Bottom line
This interim final rule applies to a bank servicer of this size and has been in effect since July 15, 2025, so it is already effective. It mostly removes COVID-19-era Regulation X provisions that the document says expired by their own terms, so the work is likely cleanup rather than new operations. The most important action is to confirm that policies, scripts, system rules, templates and foreclosure controls no longer rely on the removed COVID provisions. Also confirm how the servicer is treating the COVID-related wording that the text leaves in § 1024.41(c)(2)(v)(A)(1).

## What is changing
The IFR rescinds the 2021 COVID RESPA Rule (86 FR 34848, June 30, 2021) by amending Regulation X. The document gives two reasons.
- The temporary provisions have sunset. The additional COVID early intervention live contact requirements ended by their own terms (the preamble cites October 1, 2022). The special loss mitigation procedural safeguards did not apply to foreclosure first notices or filings made on or after January 1, 2022.
- The Bureau's policy is to streamline regulatory requirements. It says the provisions "needlessly complicate Regulation X without commensurate benefits."

Specific amendments in the regulatory text:
- **§ 1024.31:** removes the definition of "COVID-19-related hardship."
- **§ 1024.39 (early intervention):** revises paragraph (a) (live contact) and removes paragraph (e). The revised (a) requires live contact, or good faith efforts to establish it, by the 36th day of delinquency and again within 36 days after each payment due date while the borrower remains delinquent. It also requires promptly informing the borrower about loss mitigation options, if appropriate.
- **§ 1024.41 (loss mitigation):**
  - Revises (c)(2)(i). The cross-reference now reads "(c)(2)(ii), (iii), and (v)."
  - Revises (c)(2)(v)(A)(1).
  - Removes (c)(2)(vi) and (f)(3).
- **Supplement I (Official Interpretations):**
  - Revises comments 39(a) and 41(b)(1).
  - Removes comment 41(f)(3), "Temporary Special COVID-19 Loss Mitigation Procedural Safeguards," and 41(f)(3)(ii)(C), "Unresponsive Borrower."

The document does not reproduce the prior text of the revised provisions, so a line-by-line comparison against the servicer's current procedures needs the codified before-and-after text.

**Ambiguity flagged:** The preamble says the Bureau is removing "language relating to the COVID-19 pandemic." Yet the revised § 1024.41(c)(2)(v)(A)(1) as printed still refers to "COVID-19-related hardship" and to the Coronavirus Economic Stabilization Act, section 4022 (15 U.S.C. 9056). The IFR also removes the definition of "COVID-19-related hardship" from § 1024.31. The document does not explain how these fit together, and this analysis does not resolve it.

## Does it apply to this servicer?
**In scope.** Regulation X's mortgage servicing provisions apply to this servicer as a bank servicing more than 5,000 loans.
- The document does not set different requirements by institution type or portfolio size.
- It does not mention a small servicer exemption.
- The Section 1022 analysis says the rule has no unique impact on insured depository institutions or credit unions with less than $10 billion in assets. The servicer's asset size is not in the profile.
- The servicer's position relative to any small-servicer threshold is therefore not addressed in the document (see Items to verify).

## What is not changing
- The core live contact requirement stays: the 36-day timing, the "good faith efforts" standard, and promptly informing borrowers of loss mitigation options "if appropriate." The text of § 1024.39(a) and its comments is restated.
- The § 1024.39(b)(1) written notice remains referenced in the early intervention comments.
- The § 1024.41 requirement not to evade evaluation of a complete application stays. The exceptions in (c)(2)(ii), (iii) and (v) are retained by cross-reference.
- The comments on complete applications and reasonable diligence stay. These cover:
  - Servicer flexibility in setting application requirements.
  - Stopping document collection for ineligible options.
  - Treating inquiries versus applications.
  - Reasonable diligence, including after servicing transfers and during short-term forbearance or repayment plans offered under § 1024.41(c)(2)(iii).
  - Information not in the borrower's control.
- The § 1024.41(c)(2)(v)(A)(1) text remains in the rule, including its COVID-related wording (see ambiguity above).
- Provisions of Regulation X not named in the amendatory instructions are not addressed by the document.
- The 2024 proposed rule (89 FR 60204) is only described. The preamble says it would provide streamlined loss mitigation flexibility and that the Bureau would consider its comments in any future rulemaking. As described, it creates no obligation, and its current status is not stated here.
