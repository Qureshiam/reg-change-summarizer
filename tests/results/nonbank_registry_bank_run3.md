## Bottom line
This final rule rescinds the CFPB's nonbank registry rule, which applied to nonbanks and by its terms excluded insured depository institutions and insured credit unions. It is not expected to impose obligations on this servicer directly. It has been effective since October 29, 2025, so the rule itself requires no action. The one useful step is to confirm that no affiliate, subsidiary or service provider was relying on the registry requirements and that the institution's own insured status is as assumed.

## What is changing
- **Old requirement (NBR Rule, published July 8, 2024, effective September 16, 2024):** Covered nonbanks subject to certain public agency or court orders had to register with the CFPB and submit the orders and related information. Bureau-supervised registered entities also had to designate an "attesting executive" and file an annual signed written statement on order compliance. The CFPB was also authorized to publish registry information.
- **New requirement:** All of it is rescinded. The CFPB removes and reserves all of part 1092, including the registration provisions (§§ 1092.202-203), the written-statement provisions (§ 1092.204), the publication provisions (§ 1092.205), subpart A, §§ 1092.200-201 and 206, and appendix A.
- **Stated reasons:** The CFPB says the costs are not justified by speculative and unquantified benefits, that the registry duplicates other sources such as NMLS, and that it is not a necessary monitoring tool. It also cites the cost of maintaining the system.
- **Rejected alternatives:** The CFPB considered and declined partial rescission and a general nonbank registration requirement.

## Does it apply to this servicer?
**Out of scope for direct obligations, subject to the verification items below.**
- The document describes the NBR Rule and this rescission as applying to nonbank covered persons. The Registration and Publication Provisions affect covered persons that are not insured depository institutions, insured credit unions, or related persons. The document states the rule "will have no direct impacts on any insured depository institution or insured credit union."
- The servicer profile is a bank or credit union with more than 5,000 loans. The document does not set different requirements by portfolio size, and the $5 million receipts threshold applied only to Bureau-supervised nonbanks.
- **Possible indirect effects:** The document says insured depository institutions and credit unions with $10 billion or less in assets might see indirect effects, such as benefits to nonbank affiliates or competitive effects. It also notes that commenters objected to the NBR Rule's coverage of nonbank affiliates, credit union service organizations (CUSOs) and privately insured credit unions. The document does not say how those entities were treated, so affiliates and any privately insured status are items to verify.

## What is not changing
- Obligations imposed by any underlying agency or court order stay in force. The rescission removes only the registry, attestation and publication layer.
- The CFPB says it keeps its other tools, including risk-based supervision, follow-up examinations on order compliance, investigations and market monitoring.
- The document refers to NMLS reporting and MU1 disclosures as existing, separate mechanisms. It does not say they are changed.
- The document does not address mortgage servicing requirements, such as loss mitigation, early intervention, escrow or transfers, and does not change them.

## Recommended next steps
1. Confirm the institution's status as an insured depository institution or insured credit union, including whether any part is privately insured. This determines whether the exclusion applies.
2. Inventory nonbank affiliates, CUSOs and subsidiaries for any NBR registrations or attesting executive designations, and update them.
3. Retire NBR Rule items from policies, compliance calendars and regulatory change trackers.
4. Check state-level order reporting and licensing obligations (NMLS and state regulator requirements) for any related duties, since those are not addressed here.
5. Note the rescission in the regulatory change log and close out any open NBR implementation work.
