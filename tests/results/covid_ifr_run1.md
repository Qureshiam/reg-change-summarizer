## Bottom line
This interim final rule applies to a bank servicing more than 5,000 loans, as it applies to servicers generally. It has been effective since July 15, 2025, so it is already in force. It removes expired COVID-19 provisions from Regulation X and leaves the baseline 36-day live contact and loss mitigation rules in place. The most important action is to confirm that policies, scripts, templates, and system configurations no longer rely on the removed COVID-era text. Also confirm the rule's current status, since this document is only an interim final rule.

## What is changing
The rule rescinds the 2021 COVID RESPA Rule (86 FR 34848, June 30, 2021). The preamble gives two reasons:
- The temporary provisions have already sunset or expired.
- The Bureau's policy is to streamline regulatory requirements.

Specific text changes:
- **§ 1024.31:** The definition of "COVID-19-related hardship" is removed.
- **§ 1024.39 (early intervention):**
  - Paragraph (a) is revised. The text shown requires live contact, or good faith efforts to establish it, by the 36th day of delinquency and again within 36 days after each payment due date while the borrower stays delinquent. The servicer must then promptly inform the borrower about loss mitigation options, if appropriate.
  - Paragraph (e) is removed.
  - The document does not reproduce the prior text, so the exact wording removed from (a) is not shown.
- **§ 1024.41 (loss mitigation):**
  - Paragraph (c)(2)(i) is revised. The cross-reference now reads "(c)(2)(ii), (iii), and (v)."
  - Paragraph (c)(2)(v)(A)(1) is revised.
  - Paragraphs (c)(2)(vi) and (f)(3) are removed. Per the preamble, (f)(3) was the temporary special COVID-19 loss mitigation procedural safeguards.
- **Supplement I (Official Interpretations):**
  - Comments 39(a) (live contact) and 41(b)(1) (complete loss mitigation application) are revised.
  - Comments for 41(f)(3) and 41(f)(3)(ii)(C) (unresponsive borrower) are removed.

The preamble says the temporary provisions had already ended by their own terms:
- The foreclosure safeguards did not apply if the first foreclosure notice or filing was made on or after January 1, 2022.
- The temporary live contact requirements applied only until October 1, 2022.

## Does it apply to this servicer?
**In scope.** The text amends Regulation X's servicing provisions generally and does not distinguish by institution type. The profile is a bank with more than 5,000 loans.
- **Institution type:** The § 1022 analysis says the rule has no unique impact on insured depository institutions or credit unions with under $10 billion in assets. Your asset size is not provided.
- **Portfolio size:** The document contains no small servicer exemption discussion, and no size-based carve-out appears in the amended text. With more than 5,000 loans, you fall on the larger side of any such line, but the document itself does not address this (see Items to verify).
- **Practical effect:** The preamble states borrowers and servicers are no longer using the removed safeguards, and that the rule imposes no costs. Most of the effect is likely cleanup of legacy COVID-era content.

## What is not changing
- The core live contact requirement stays in § 1024.39(a), with its 36-day timing and the duty to promptly inform borrowers about loss mitigation options if appropriate.
- The written notice under § 1024.39(b)(1) is referenced in the revised comments and is not shown as amended.
- The § 1024.41 provisions not named in the amendments are not changed. These include (c)(2)(ii), (c)(2)(iii), and (c)(2)(v) generally.
- The revised comments reaffirm existing interpretations on delinquency, what counts as live contact, good faith efforts, and the relationship between live contact and loss mitigation procedures. They also reaffirm interpretations on complete applications, reasonable diligence, and information outside the borrower's control.
- The July 24, 2024 proposal (89 FR 60204) is only a proposal and creates no obligation. The preamble says the Bureau would consider its comments in any future rulemaking.
